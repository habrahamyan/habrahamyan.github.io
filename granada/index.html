granada

Exact, symbolic algebra for perturbation-theory integrands: from an integrand written on paper to master integrals with rational coefficients.

  • v0.1.0
  • Python ≥ 3.10
  • depends only on SymPy
  • MIT licence

What it does

Loop integrals in cosmological perturbation theory start out as products of kernels, power spectra, scalar products of 3-vectors and spherical Bessel functions $j_\ell(|\boldsymbol v|\,r)$ of the separation $r$. Getting from such an integrand to a short list of master integrals takes pages of tensor algebra and bookkeeping. granada does that algebra exactly: a vector is a name, a scalar product is a pair of names, and exponents are rationals or linear forms in the spectral index $n$. Nothing in it is numerical.

It consists of a shared core and two independent pipelines:

  • The core (granada): the notation, the kernels $F_n, G_n$ and $P_L$, the reduction to masters (reduce, align, fold), the average over the direction of $\boldsymbol r$, and output as LaTeX/HTML pages or Mathematica files.
  • The Feynman pipeline (granada.feynman): takes a term with an axis $\hat{\boldsymbol z}$ through peel → project → average to scalar integrands sorted by powers of $\nu = \hat{\boldsymbol z}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol r}$, then evaluates them at your master integral.
  • The Schwinger pipeline (granada.schwinger): writes Fourier-type loop integrals as Schwinger parameters acting on one Gaussian $Z(A,J)$, and carries the terms that factorize to closed forms.
  • Tensor decomposition (granada.decompose): writes a rank-$n$ tensor as a sum over products of $\delta_{ij}$ and $\hat r_i$. It stands on its own.

Every step can be rendered as a page, so each stage can be checked against a calculation by hand.

from granada import Vector, G2, PL, g
from granada.feynman import feynmanReduce

k, p = Vector("k"), Vector("p")
L = feynmanReduce(G2(k, p) * PL(k) * PL(p) * g(k) * g(p) * g(k + p),
                  path="feynman.html")   # one block per stage, to check by hand

Why I made it

I wrote granada during my master's thesis on the generating function of pairwise velocity differences in large-scale structure. The loop integrals there carry velocity kernels, spherical Bessel functions of the separation and powers of the line-of-sight angle, and reducing them by hand meant long stretches of tensor algebra where a single dropped sign could go unnoticed for weeks.

I wanted a tool that takes an integrand exactly as I would write it on paper, shows every intermediate step so it can be checked by eye, and hands back a list of masters with exact coefficients, ready for Mathematica. It grew into two pipelines because the Feynman and Schwinger approaches each handle a different class of integrals well.

Install

pip install granada

For inline rendering in Jupyter notebooks: pip install "granada[notebook]".
